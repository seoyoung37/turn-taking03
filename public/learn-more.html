The problem

Online calls lose
the in-between moments.

In a physical room you can feel when someone is about to speak through a lean forward, a glance toward the current speaker, a breath. Online, that signal disappears. People talk over each other, silences feel awkward, and quieter voices never find their moment.

InBetween restores that layer by reading open mouth combined with body signals, and making them visible to everyone in the call.

Pre-speech signals

Open mouth.
Two different signals.

An open mouth on its own does nothing, it's just the prerequisite. What InBetween reads is what the body does at the same time as the mouth opens.

Open mouth + Gazing at speaker
Directional pull

When you open your mouth AND look toward whoever is currently speaking, your tile subtly inflates in their direction. The edge facing the speaker rises slightly, just a lean toward them. It signals "I'm listening, I might step in."

→

Gazer's right edge inflates toward the speaker

↑Speaker above → top edge rises
↓Speaker below → tile stays flat
←Speaker left → left edge rises
→Speaker right → right edge rises
Open mouth + Leaning forward
Bottom rises first

When you open your mouth AND lean your body toward the camera, InBetween reads this as a strong turn-claiming signal. The bottom of your tile where your mouth appears, swings toward the viewer first, as if your chin is leading the way in. This means "I'm about to speak."

→

Top stays anchored — bottom (mouth area) swings toward viewer

While gazing signals connection, leaning signals intent. The tile's upward motion from the bottom mirrors the physical act of leaning in to speak.

Microphone state

Mute is
the gatekeeper.

If you're muted, neither signal fires, because you can't actually speak. Only when unmuted does InBetween begin reading mouth + body signals. The system respects your intent and only surfaces cues that are meaningful.

Gaze pull and leaning signals are active

Muted

No speaker mode

Silence becomes
an opening.

After 5 seconds of no one speaking, InBetween shifts modes. All tiles go upright and rearrange into a slowly rotating circle, split by how much each person has spoken.

5s
of silence

Outer ring — spoke more

Participants who've spoken the most sit on the outer ring.

Inner ring — spoke less

Those who've said less move inward — a gentle cue that the floor is open to them.

Slowly rotating

The arrangement rotates until someone speaks. No one is fixed at center.
